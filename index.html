Why AuthorVeyra exists

Because an author's challenges rarely come with ready-made answers.

An author may know what they want to accomplish without knowing exactly what is preventing progress or what needs to happen next.

A book may struggle to reach its intended readers. A publishing decision may involve unfamiliar choices. An author's wider plans may require several connected areas of work.

These situations are different. They deserve to be understood on their own terms rather than treated with the same predetermined response.

That is the thinking behind AuthorVeyra: understand the author's situation first, then determine a considered way forward.

THE AUTHORVEYRA PRINCIPLE

Understanding the challenges

What is standing between you and what you want to achieve?

The difficulty may concern one book, your publishing decisions or a much wider ambition. Here are examples of situations authors may need help working through.

01 / REACHING READERS

Your book is not reaching the right audience

You have published your work, but the readers most likely to value it are not discovering it or recognising why it matters to them.

02 / CLARITY

Your book is not communicating its value clearly

The way your book is presented may not fully communicate its purpose, intended readership, distinctive value or relevance.

03 / NEXT STEPS

You are unsure what to do next

You have ideas and ambitions for your work, but need to understand which opportunities deserve attention and where to begin.

04 / PUBLISHING DECISIONS

You are facing unfamiliar publishing decisions

You may be considering new markets, publishing arrangements, distribution options or other decisions that require careful evaluation.

05 / WIDER AMBITIONS

Your ambitions extend beyond one book

Your catalogue, expertise, creative ideas or future plans may hold opportunities that need a more connected approach.

06 / COMPLEX PROJECTS

Your plans involve several moving parts

You may need to bring together different decisions, people, capabilities and activities to move a publishing project forward.

A wider understanding

Sometimes the real challenge is bigger than the book.

What appears to be a problem with one part of an author's work may be connected to something much wider.

A book that is not reaching its intended audience may require a closer look at its positioning and presentation. A publishing ambition may involve market research, distribution decisions or suitable professional relationships.

We consider the wider circumstances where they matter. This helps us avoid treating an isolated symptom as though it were the entire problem.

The aim is to understand the full situation before deciding what should happen next.

A WIDER VIEW OF AUTHOR NEEDS

How we approach each situation

We understand first. Then we work out the way forward.

We do not assume that every author needs the same response. Our approach is shaped by the challenge, the author's objectives and what the situation requires.

01 / LISTEN

Understand your situation

We begin with your ambitions, concerns, current circumstances and what you hope to make possible.

02 / EXAMINE

Identify what needs attention

We examine the relevant details, consider the underlying issues and distinguish immediate problems from wider opportunities.

03 / DEVELOP

Work out a practical response

We consider possible ways forward and identify an approach that reflects your actual objectives.

04 / AGREE

Establish what happens next

We clarify the proposed work, responsibilities, expected deliverables, costs and any relevant limitations before proceeding.

05 / ACT

Move the agreed work forward

Depending on the project, we may provide guidance, carry out agreed work directly or coordinate appropriate specialists.

06 / REVIEW

Maintain a clear working relationship

We communicate about progress, address relevant questions and review the next steps within the agreed scope.

Understand our approach
Clarity and responsibility

You should understand what you are agreeing to.

Good working relationships depend on clear expectations, honest communication and an understanding of responsibilities.

Before an agreed project begins, the intended work, scope, deliverables, costs and relevant responsibilities should be clear.

Where a project requires external specialists or other providers, their involvement and respective responsibilities should also be explained before arrangements are made.

We believe authors should be able to make informed decisions about the work being proposed and the people involved.
