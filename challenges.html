Understanding Author Challenges

What is standing between your work and what you want it to achieve?

Authors can face challenges at many different stages of their journey. A book may struggle to reach its intended readers. A publishing opportunity may be difficult to navigate. A growing author business may involve decisions that are not easy to manage alone. Understanding the real challenge is the first step towards finding a meaningful way forward.

Books, readers and visibility

Challenges affecting your book and its connection with readers

Creating a valuable book is one achievement. Helping the right readers discover it, understand its value and decide to engage with it involves a different set of considerations.

Sometimes the difficulty is not the quality of the work itself, but how the book is positioned, presented, discovered or connected with its intended audience.

01 / DISCOVERABILITY

Readers are not discovering your book

Your book may be difficult for its intended audience to find. Its categories, keywords, metadata, subject positioning or online presence may not be connecting it with the readers most likely to be interested.

02 / READER INTEREST

People notice the book but do not take the next step

Attention does not always lead to interest or purchases. The book's description, cover presentation, positioning or supporting information may not communicate its value clearly enough to prospective readers.

03 / AUTHOR POSITIONING

Your expertise is not fully reflected in your presentation

Your professional experience, knowledge, ideas or background may not be communicated effectively alongside your books, making it harder for readers to understand your perspective and credibility.

04 / CATALOGUE GROWTH

Your books are not creating connections with one another

Related titles, book series, backlist publications or complementary subjects may be presented separately, leaving opportunities for readers to discover more of your work unexplored.

05 / CREATIVE FORMATS

Your work could reach audiences in different ways

A book may have potential beyond its original format. Depending on its subject and audience, audio, video, animation, visual storytelling or adaptation may offer other ways for people to experience the work.

06 / MARKETING DIRECTION

You are unsure what deserves attention first

There may be several possible marketing and growth opportunities, but no clear understanding of which issue is most important or which next step is likely to make the greatest practical difference.

Publishing, commercial and operational challenges

Challenges that extend beyond the book itself

An author's ambitions may involve publishing decisions, new markets, professional relationships, commercial development and the practical requirements of managing a wider publishing project.

These challenges can involve several connected decisions. Understanding how they relate to one another can help authors consider their options more clearly.

07 / PUBLISHING DECISIONS

You need clarity about your publishing direction

You may be weighing different publishing routes, production arrangements or commercial considerations without knowing which direction best fits your objectives and circumstances.

08 / INTERNATIONAL OPPORTUNITIES

You want to introduce your work to new markets

Reaching readers in another country can involve understanding local audiences, market requirements, publishing arrangements, potential partners and practical routes into that market.

09 / BOOK DISTRIBUTION

Making your book available to readers is difficult

Distribution, availability, ordering arrangements, shipping and fulfilment can create obstacles between a published book and the readers or markets it is intended to reach.

10 / PROFESSIONAL CONNECTIONS

You are finding it difficult to identify the right partners

Your publishing ambitions may depend on relationships with publishers, distributors, retailers, organisations or other professionals, but identifying suitable connections and possible arrangements can be challenging.

11 / AUTHOR BUSINESS GROWTH

Your wider commercial opportunities remain unexplored

Your books, knowledge, professional experience or intellectual property may offer opportunities beyond individual book sales. Understanding how those opportunities could develop requires a wider perspective.

12 / PROJECT COORDINATION

Different parts of your project are difficult to bring together

When a project involves multiple professionals, suppliers, decisions or activities, unclear responsibilities and disconnected plans can make progress more complicated.

Looking beyond the obvious

The visible problem may not be the only problem.

When a book is not reaching its intended audience, the immediate reaction may be to increase promotion. But the underlying issue could involve its positioning, discoverability, availability or the way its value is communicated to readers.

Similarly, an author planning to enter a new market may need to consider more than finding new readers. Publishing arrangements, distribution, local requirements and suitable professional relationships may all influence what is realistically possible.

Looking at the wider situation helps distinguish the symptoms of a problem from the factors that may actually be contributing to it.
